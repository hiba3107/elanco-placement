Learn more about the seasonal verdict

A chi-squared test compares the monthly counts with a perfectly even spread. The page only calls it a seasonal pattern when the months differ by more than chance would explain, at the usual 5% threshold. With fewer than 60 sightings it says there are too few to judge.

    Learn more about getting a tick identified

    Unfed ticks range from under 1 mm to about 4 mm. The UK Health Security Agency's Tick Surveillance Scheme identifies ticks sent in by the public. It does not test them for disease. Keep the tick in a sealed container somewhere cool until you post it.

    Preventing tick bites

    Five simple habits for you and your pets.

    General guidance, not medical advice. Sources: NHS and UKHSA.

    1. Cover up before you go out

      • Long sleeves, and trousers tucked into socks. Light colours show ticks.
      • Use a tick repellent, such as one with DEET.
      • Keep to paths, away from long grass and bracken.

    2. Check yourself when you get back

      • Armpits, groin, waistband, behind the knees, hairline and ears.
      • Brush off your clothes outdoors, and shower soon after.

    3. Check your pets

      • Head, ears, neck, armpits and between the toes, after every walk.
      • Dogs that explore badger setts or fox earths need a closer look.
      • Ask your vet which tick treatment suits your pet.

    4. Remove ticks quickly

      • Grip it close to the skin with fine-tipped tweezers or a tick removal tool.
      • Pull upwards slowly and firmly, or as the tool directs. Don't squeeze it.
      • Never burn it or cover it in oil or alcohol. Clean the bite afterwards.

    5. Know when to get help

      • Call your GP or NHS 111 if a red rash spreads around a bite, or you get flu-like symptoms, up to 3 months later.
      • Not everyone gets a rash, so mention any recent tick bite.
      • Call your vet if your pet seems unwell after a bite.

    Data quality

    Good to know

    • Markers sit at each city's centre, because the data names a city, not a place.
    • Recency is measured from the newest record (December 2024), not today.
    • The passerine tick. Elanco's data calls this Dermacentor frontalis. The UK government's tick species list (UKHSA) names the passerine tick Ixodes frontalis.
    • Every species appears in every city all year, unlike real UK records, so treat this as sample data.
